Start Designing for Excess Management

  • UX
  • Business centric design
  • Dec 12, 2018
  • 5 Min read
Share this article

Everywhere, you can see a lot of PR from companies, talking about ease and convenience. Products and services ranging from mobile phones to online content, talk about how easy they are making the life for consumers. What is key to watch out for, is that within this promise also lies the construct to persuade consumers for excess. At subliminal levels, the habit of excess is being reinforced, such that consumers want more and more. To make consumers buy things they don’t need, they need to be trained.

Consumers are being trained for excess in many ways…

Accelerating Stimulation

Stimulation and building a habit around craving for more has been one of the oldest marketing tricks. Televised content have for years been doing this. The challenge is that consumers get desensitized to stimulation, and competition is getting tougher, creating the need for even stronger and more addictive forms for stimulation. The television has been increasingly upping the level on how visceral is the content that they have been broadcasting. Content players and now OTT companies are constantly pushing this boundary and building strongly addicted consumers who thrive on such content.

Content viewing is getting more private with each family member viewing their own private content through their personal peephole. Technology around rendering extremely realistic digital humans has also significantly advanced. With AR coming in, viewing stimulating content will go to the next level with consumers viewing content of all nature even in public spaces.

Food offered on app platforms is far more tantalizing than what you could cook at home. You don’t even have to wait to go to a restaurant. People will soon stop making food at home and will subscribe to monthly food plans that bring in new flavors every day. In short, across various services and products, we have trained the consumer’s brain to crave for constant stimulation.

Instant Gratification

Consumer behavior has been greatly altered to expect instant gratification from all services. Starting with content where they don’t have to wait for the next episode, even for a day and can binge watch a whole series worth of content.

Everything is available at a click. From clothes to food and even humans, everything is like a flavor. Its all about trying out new tantalizing experiences and then move on to the next. Consumers no longer have to go through the trouble of courtship during a relationship. Getting bored with your current partner? Just tap on tinder and find a whole new flavor of human that you want.

In fact why bother to go through the challenges of interacting with a human, just buy a robot.

Life Instagrammed

The need for individuals to be perceived as special and successful is especially evident on platforms such as Instagram. Here consumers are mesmerized by the lives of others and how pale their life appears in contrast. They spend a lot of time and effort showcasing how great their lives are in turn. This recursive loop creates the prevalent feeling of inadequacy and the strong need to get the new & more & more.

Don’t wait, get Debt!

To solve the income gap, companies are finding alternate ways of offering credit to consumers who would not have qualified if they were gauged in traditional ways. Credit comes to the rescue where consumption far exceeds the individuals earning power. This is especially the case since most millennials are not earning anywhere close to the life they aspire to live.

Credit companies are training consumers to reduce inhibitions, be less fearful and get habituated to use credit. With not much to show as a guarantee, consumers are handing over all forms of personal data as a way to get credit.

The Ideal Consumer

Companies have successfully built the ideal consumer. One who is addicted to stimulation and wants increasing quantities of it. One who has low self-esteem and is never satisfied with their current state. One who wants the new thing, right now!

This coupled with bad financial management, is a perfectly trained consumer for any brand to push their wares to.

Now, this article is not a sermon on impending doom of the capitalist society but a context setting for the considerations you might have to design for. With excess comes the baggage of environmental, psychological and financial impact on consumers.

It is time when companies start designing to manage excess. You may even find the next set of startups focussed on excess management. Start thinking about how would ‘excess’ start affecting your target audience and how would you start addressing it through design. Here are some examples…

Addiction management: Phycological impact of excess has definitely become more visible. Anxiety disorders are rampant and manifesting in all forms.

Financial management

Debt amongst millennials is increasing at an alarming rate. Awareness and behavior change are key design objectives to address this.
