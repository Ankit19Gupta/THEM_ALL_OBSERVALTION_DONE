‘Addictive Technology’ the Only Winning Product Strategy?

  • UX
  • Business centric design
  • Aug 17, 2018
  • 5 Min read
Share this article

When asked the question about what would make them succeed in the future, I am intrigued by what most organizations making products and services tend to tout as their secret sauce. From the euphoric cry that they will be the biggest, largest, richest market leader to chest thumping on technology stacks and that ‘voice interaction capability’ or cramming everything into chat agents will be It. By all means I mean no disrespect, but to me this seems incredibly foggy as a strategy for success. To say that IOT or Big Data or Voice or Smart Watches will do it, is like saying that we have a lot of things that we don’t clearly know how to use or what they will be for, but somehow we will survive and do better.

I am of the opinion that if success is to be found, one needs to look at the aspirations of the human animal how they manifest into behavior, which becomes the petri dish for new products and services. At the centre of these aspirations lies the human brain and how it entirely influences our perception of everything. The brain is a fantastic thing, doing incredible things for us, firing neurons and performing complex tasks. Scientists have long studied the functioning of the human brain and have deciphered many amazing abilities, and many more remain to be uncovered. What we have learnt has lent an irrevocable understanding that everything companies strive for, is around winning the human brain.

Organizations have been exposed to brain science by functions such as Marketing, User Experience etc. Their focus has been to create products and services that rely on converting customers into ‘Dopamine Addicts’. They understand that the brain secretes Dopamine as a reward mechanism for various tasks performed. Releasing of Dopamine is also pleasurable for the brain and it craves more and more of it. We have whole industries built around selling products or services to people that make their brains secrete more dopamine such that they want more of their products and services. Look at ecommerce, now at its incredible magnificence, permeating our daily lives with all the goodies which brings Christmas/Diwali/etc daily to our eager doorsteps. We don’t need most of the stuff we buy but we are hooked into a loop of buying more and more things with the ever decreasing guilt of ‘did we really need that’. Shopping online now, has nothing to do with actual need but a strong desire from our brain to be entertained. It craves Dopamine and shopping for an assortment of tantalizing things produces a lot of it. Ecommerce is in the business of entertainment and constant stimulation of the brain. What about mobile phones? I am quite certain that while you read this note, there would be at least a few instances where you jump to your email, Whatsapp or Facebook on your phone. Most people to my knowledge do not receive a constant stream of ‘extremely important’ communication that they can not refrain from attending, at any cost. It seems that the anticipation of getting communication on these channels, even if it’s only spam, also produces Dopamine and we crave this every minute. These services are in the business of constant stimulation of the brain. Binge watching sitcoms is now a common thing to do. Hours are spent watching one episode after the other and then crave for some more. Most forms of online or televised entertainment cause the brain to secrete copious amounts of Dopamine. They too are in the business of stimulation of the human brain. It is also important to understand that humans get desensitized to forms of stimulation. What might be all the rage today may be the mundane of tomorrow. Businesses are thus focussed on providing alternate and increased amounts of stimulation to be differentiated and to win in the market. I hope this illustrates that humanity at large is in an era where stimulation is the key drive and most businesses are focussed on winning on this.

Companies focusing on ever increasing stimulation for humanity can not last. Our biological system is legacy, in fact it has hardly evolved from the time we were roaming around in the Savanah. Quite a few of our brain functions and abilities remain from the time we had to fend or flee from predators of the animal kind. Technology on the other hand has accelerated, and incredibly so. Moore’s law, that predicted that evolution of key aspects of technology would accelerate at an exponential pace, has already been outpaced. We are at a point of the human civilization where our biology is clearly not a match for technology in most aspects and the others are soon to be scaled. Our brains are already feeling the effects of over-stimulation, our eyes suffer from fatigue staring at brightly lit screens that over stimulate the cells on our retinas. Still every evening is spent in not de-stressing from a full day of screens but in exposure to more on-demand stimulation. Mental function challenges, social disconnect, depression is now running rampant amongst the populace. People are starting to look for escape from this lifestyle, constructed by the commercial world. More and more people are seeking a reprieve from the ‘daily’ and joining spiritual programs which focus on doing things the natural way. Vacations are often focussed on being technology free or going to places where connectivity is a challenge. What this change in behavior hints towards is the beginning of an era where humans want to focus more on living and then spending lesser time slaved to stimulation. This would start to influence how companies and services approach their customers in a big way. They will have to work extra hard to push cumbersome interactions with technology to the backend where users don’t have to worry about them. They will have to focus more on providing experiences that comprise a full life than push consumerism on cars, real estate, electronics and other things. All in all companies will undergo a major shift from their current model of peddling stimulation & addiction.

This is one perspective that illustrates how looking at lifestyles and aspirations provide a much more robust model of building successful businesses than pure focus on technology traversing the hype cycle. Think about how lifestyles give form to aspirations & behavior. Look for how these core Thems could define what your customers would look for from products and services. And lastly don’t forget that these evolve over time.
